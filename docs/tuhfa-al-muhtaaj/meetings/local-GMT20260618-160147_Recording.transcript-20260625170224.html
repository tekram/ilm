Ijāra for Worship Acts: Jihad, Prayer Leadership, Quran Recitation & The Prophet's Grave — June 18, 2026

📅 2026-06-18 🕐 16:01

Overview

This Tuḥfat al-Muḥtāj session opens with a preliminary exchange on a contemporary question (touching a smartphone Quran app without wuḍūʾ), then enters the main text's chapter on ijāra for acts of worship — specifically those for which hiring is invalid because they require personal niyya, versus those where hiring is valid. The session closes with a Q&A exchange on the conditions for wiping over khuff (leather socks).

Preliminary: Touching a Smartphone with a Quran App Without Wuḍūʾ

A scholarly exchange on whether it is prohibited (ḥarām) under the Shāfiʿī school to touch a smartphone that has a Quran application without ritual purity. Arguments for permissibility:

1. Cut-and-paste text is not kitāba in the ʿurf sense. A note in Ḥāshiyat ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd states that Quranic text formed by cutting and pasting letters is not considered "writing" (kitāba ʿurfan), and thus does not trigger the prohibition on touching. The argument: a phone's pixels constitute a display, not an inscription. The Quran letters on a screen are not written on the device in the way ink is written on paper; one can delete them from software but cannot physically erase them from a surface.

2. Analogy with tafsir books. Tafsir books may be touched without wuḍūʾ because they are not called "Quran" — the commentary predominates over the Quranic text and the whole is named tafsīr. By analogy, a phone is not called a muṣḥaf.

3. Default permissibility (barāʾat al-dhimma). Prohibition is a ruling that requires a positive legal basis (yaqīn aw qiyās ʿalā manṣūṣ). The default is permissibility; those who hold the act prohibited carry the burden of proof.

4. Scholars' positions: Ḥabīb ʿUmar ibn Ḥāmid al-Jaylānī and Shaykh Muḥammad al-Khaṭīb and those with them leaned toward prohibition; some of them on grounds of taʿẓīm (reverence for the Quran) rather than strict qiyās. Ḥabīb ʿUmar ibn Ḥafīẓ and Sayyid Ḥusayn al-Saqqāf leaned toward permissibility. It was also reported — though not definitively confirmed — that the Syrian Mufti Shaykh Usāma al-Rifāʿī had revised an earlier prohibition fatwa.

Main Lesson: Ijāra for Acts of Worship

The General Rule

Hiring (ijāra) is invalid for any act of worship that requires a personal intention (niyya). The rationale: the purpose of such worship is to test the mukallaf (ikhtibār al-mukallaf) through personal obedience and self-mortification (kasr al-nafs). No substitute can fulfill this purpose in place of the obligated person. The agent (ajīr) therefore earns no wage even if he performs the act while expecting payment (māl ṭāmiʿan), because kullu mā lā yaṣiḥḥu al-istijār lahu lā ujra li-fāʿilih wa-in ʿamila ṭāmiʿan.

Jihad

Hiring a Muslim to perform jihad is invalid, even if he intends thereby to uphold this emblematic act of Islam and directs the proceeds to Islamic causes. Reasoning: when the enemy is present, jihad becomes personally obligatory (farḍ ʿayn) on those present; the hired Muslim's action occurs for himself, not as an agent substituting for the employer. A dhimmī may be hired for jihad — but only by the Imam and in accordance with the relevant chapter — since the dhimmī has no personal obligation of jihad.

Prayer Leadership (Imāma)

Hiring for prayer leadership is invalid, even for supererogatory prayers (nawāfil), because the imām performs the prayer for himself. Anyone who wishes may follow him in congregation, whether or not he intended imāma. The benefit of congregational prayer (faḍīlat al-jamāʿa) accrues to the congregation only if the imām made the niyya for leadership; this is a benefit that belongs exclusively to the imām-prayer and cannot be transferred to a mustaʾjir through a hire contract. Khutba (the sermon) is analogous: it requires personal performance and cannot be delegated for wages.

Exception noted: Some said khutba is like imāma in this regard (al-Ramlī's apparent position), but the author of the Tuḥfa notes that what follows in the text appears to contradict this and suggests the rājiḥ is that khutba is not like imāma.

Adhān

The adhān does not require a niyya, so hiring for it is valid. The wage covers the full act of calling the adhān together with observing the prayer time (maʿa naw raʿāyat al-waqt).

Visiting the Prophet's Grave ﷺ

Three categories of acts at the grave, each with its own ruling:

1. The visit itself (al-ziyāra li-l-wuqūf wa-l-mushāhada):

Invalid for both ijāra and jiʿāla. The visit requires niyya — merely being physically present at the grave without the intention of visiting does not constitute a ziyāra. Al-Māwardī and others explicitly held this. The visit of other graves is a fortiori more clearly invalid (the Prophet's grave being the primary case). Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Aṣbaḥī held the opposite (valid hire for visiting), but the preferred view (al-rājiḥ) follows al-Māwardī.

2. Supplication (duʿāʾ) at the grave:

Valid for ijāra if the specific content of the duʿāʾ is stipulated in the contract (ʿayyanu mā yadʿū bih). If the content is not specified, ijāra is invalid (since it requires a defined deliverable), but a jiʿāla is valid (since jiʿāla tolerates unknowns). Rationale: supplication admits of delegation (taqbalu al-niyāba).

3. Conveying the salutation (iblag al-salām):

Both ijāra and jiʿāla are valid. Conveying a greeting is a form of agency that clearly admits of substitution.

Quran recitation at the grave, with duʿāʾ afterward:

Valid, whether or not a specific time or place is stipulated, whether or not the reward is dedicated to the mustaʾjir, and whether or not he is physically present. Rationale: the grave is a site of baraka and the descent of mercy; supplication after recitation is closer to being answered; holding the mustaʾjir present in the heart during recitation draws the descending mercy upon him.

Dedicating recitation/reward to the Prophet ﷺ:

Permissible — indeed mustahabb — to dedicate the reward (thawāb) of one's recitation to the Prophet ﷺ, augmenting his honor. This was affirmed by a group of later scholars and is not the "mistake" some have imagined. The Prophet ﷺ receives a share of the reward of every act of obedience performed by his entire umma, multiplied through every chain of transmission, with each tier of the chain multiplying above the one after it.

Teaching and Teaching Quran

Hiring for Quran instruction (all or part) is valid, even if the mustaʾjir is the very person obligated to teach it, on the basis of the authentic hadith: "Inna aḥaqqa mā akhadhтum ʿalayhi ajran Kitāb Allāh" — "the most deserving thing for which you may take wages is the Book of Allah." The author of the Tuḥfa flags this explicitly given the large number of hadiths that on their face appear to prohibit it, which he addresses in a separate monograph on the rules of Quran teachers.

Teaching other sciences: Valid only if the specific student and the specific subject are identified in the contract.

Q&A: Conditions for Wiping over Khuff

(with Tashfeen Ekram)

Why does the duration differ between traveler (3 days/nights) and resident (1 day/night)?

The Shāfiʿī position: the traveler's practical reliance on footwear is greater given the demands of travel. Resident: a day and night covers his ordinary comings and goings (market, mosque, etc.). Traveler: a full three days accounts for the heavier footwear demands of travel. (This is the scholars' explanatory reason; wa-Llāhu aʿlam whether it is precisely correct.)

Strength requirement (imkān mutābaat al-mashy):

The khuff must be strong enough to permit continuous walking (yumkinu mutābaat al-mashy ʿalayhi). A woven sock that does not impede water — the manẓūm that does not block water — does not satisfy the Shāfiʿī condition (though schools differ on woven footwear; the key agreed point is that it must be robust enough to walk on continuously).

Certainty vs. conjecture:

Since wiping over khuff is a legal concession (rukhṣa), its conditions must be met with certainty (yaqīn), not mere conjecture — just as one may not shorten prayers (qaṣr) unless certain the minimum travel distance has been reached. If in doubt whether one's khuff is strong enough, the aṣl is that the condition is not met, and wiping is not valid. (There is a possible argument that ẓann suffices in some rukhṣa contexts, but on the principle of the rules aṣl is certainty.)

Assessment at each wearing:

The strength condition is assessed at each individual wearing, not at the time of original purchase. If a pair of socks that was perfectly sound when purchased has worn down over two years to the point where continuous walking is impossible, then:

  • Prayers performed after the socks became too weak are invalid and must be repeated.
  • Specifically: only the prayers about which the wearer is certain (yatayaqqan) that the khuff was already weakened at the time of performance must be repeated. If there is doubt about when exactly the deterioration occurred, the default (aṣl) is that the khuff was valid in the earlier period.
