Waqf Interpretation, Nadhr, Hajj Symposium Rulings & Brain Death - May 19, 2026

📅 2026-05-19 🕐 15:58

Overview

A fatāwā-assembly session covering several distinct cases. It opens with a long question on the interpretation of a waqf instrument — the force of the conjunction wāw in waqf wording, whether sequential numbering of trustees implies order, and the scope of the supervisors' authority. The session then moves to the strength of nadhr (vow) as a transfer mechanism and the conditions that invalidate it. It then surveys the resolutions of the Sixth Fiqh Symposium of the Indian Fiqh Academy (held at Dār al-ʿUlūm, Maẓhar Fūr, Aʿẓam Garh, March–April 2007) on (a) the timing of stoning the Jamarāt, (b) the reality of brain death and disconnecting life support, (c) mercy killing, and (d) network/MLM marketing — with the lecturer noting where these resolutions diverge from the Shāfiʿī position. Closing questions cover takbīr in the first ten days of Dhū al-Ḥijja, congregational voluntary night prayers, and prostration after the prescribed prayers.

1. Interpretation of a Waqf Instrument

Background / Question

A man endows ("I, the poor servant of God, son of so-and-so, have set in waqf and locked up all that I own…") his entire estate inherited from his father, plus property received by nadhr, plus all he owns in a named locality — palms, dhubūr (cultivated land plots), houses, real-estate, furniture, "everything called wealth," animate and inanimate, old and new — upon "my children and my children's children, however they propagate, and upon my wife so-and-so during her lifetime." He then directs that the yield of a named well (and others) be divided in halves: one half to repair "our house in the named locality," the other half divided among the heirs, "the male like the share of two females." He names three supervisors: number 1 his son, number 2 his wife, number 3 his daughter. The questioner asks: does the wāw here mean simultaneous joint sharing or sequential succession? Does the post-clause "and I have made the yield…" tilt the reading? Does "I have made the supervisors over the ṣalāḥ of that…" refer to the whole waqf or just the house? Do the numbered designations entail order between the supervisors?

Ruling

  • If the endower is sound, legally responsible, and free, the waqf is valid in all that he owns in the named locality. If made during maraḍ al-mawt (terminal illness), it is valid only in the legal one-third.
  • The yield is divided into halves: one half to upkeep the named house, the other half among the beneficiaries in the 2:1 ratio for males vs. females, and this 2:1 distribution is carried through all generations, not just the endower's direct heirs. The phrase "between the heirs" is not the technical legal heirs of the endower; it refers to the customary inheritance ratio.
  • The wāw in "my children and my children's children, however they propagate, and my wife…" is for absolute joining (muṭlaq al-jamʿ), as is its original meaning — i.e., joint sharing — not order. Abū Makhrama clarified this in a parallel case. Al-ʿAbbādī's view that wāw means order is weak, even though al-Māwardī said the majority hold so.
  • The post-clause "and I have made what is yielded…" is not a contextual indicator (qarīna) that overrides this. Even if it were such a qarīna, it would still not override, because the school does not even treat "generation after generation" or "lineage after lineage" — which is explicit in sequence — as sequence (since baʿd can mean "maʿa", "with"; the Qur'an: "wa-l-arḍa baʿda dhālika daḥāhā" is read as "maʿa"). All the more so for the questioner's weaker indication.
  • On the phrase "I have made the supervisors over the ṣalāḥ of that": two readings.
  • First, the phrase is explicit in installing them as full superintendents (nāẓir) over the whole waqf — discharging supervision, maintaining the asset, leasing, distributing yield, and so on. On this reading no intention is needed.
  • Second, it is metaphorical, because local custom uses ṣalāḥ and ʿimāra for upkeep only, not for asset-preservation and distribution. On this reading intention is required.
  • On the first reading or the second with intention, does the demonstrative pronoun dhālik refer to the entire waqf or just the house? The grammatical rule "the definite repeated is the same as the first; the indefinite repeated is different" (illustrated by maʿa l-ʿusri yusran) supports limiting it to the house. The Shaykh's preferred view, however, is that it refers to the whole, taking ṣalāḥ broadly.
  • Practical recommendation: in either case, the three named supervisors should obtain a confirming appointment from the local court — by way of certainty and precaution — and treat the numbered designations (nimr 1, nimr 2, nimr 3) as binding order among themselves, since in the local convention of that language those numerals are explicit in sequencing (like thumma).

Referenced scholars / texts

Abū Makhrama, al-ʿAbbādī, al-Māwardī, Jamʿ al-Jawāmiʿ (the 27th issue: al-wāw li-muṭlaq al-jamʿ; objections that it is for order, or for accompaniment), al-Suyūṭī's verse rendering, the Alfiyya, the Tuḥfa tradition on waqf wording, the Shaykh's own analysis.

2. Nadhr (Vow) as a Mode of Transfer

Background / Question

The waqf question above mentioned that some of the endower's wealth had been received "by nadhr". A student asks why nadhr is used in such cases.

Ruling

  • Nadhr is the strongest form of transferring ownership — stronger than sale, gift, will, and even waqf (the latter is debated on whether acceptance is required; for nadhr it is agreed acceptance is not required).
  • Most important advantage: valid in both the known and the unknown. A nadhr of "all that comes to me" is valid; a gift of "all that comes to me" is invalid for jahāla.
  • It is also widely used as a fallback security in modern land sales. After a sale of a plot, the parties add: "and as a precaution, so-and-so vows to so-and-so all of this land and what is in it, and so-and-so vows to so-and-so the sum of X." If the sale fails, the vow stands; if the sale stands, the vow does not take effect.
  • A nadhr may be rejected by the named recipient and so revert to the vower's ownership.
  • The nadhr is invalidated by:
  • Stating an exchange-value: "I vow to you this in exchange for that" — the vow becomes a muʿāwaḍa and is void, because nadhr must be tabarruʿ.
  • An explicit condition of return: "I vow to you this on condition that you serve me" — void.
  • A nadhr may be suspended on a condition by way of taʿlīq (cause, not exchange): "if you serve me a year, I have vowed to you X" — valid, because the lām in "li-khidmatihi lī" is causal.
  • On ibrāʾ of mahr in exchange for a share of the marital house: "if you release me from my mahr, then half the house is yours" — this is a suspended transfer of ownership, which is not a recognized form unless cast as either a muʿāwaḍa (exchange) or a taʿlīq nadhr.
  • The explicit phrases (ṣarīḥ) of nadhr include "lillāhi ʿalayya kadhā" and the muʿtamad also recognizes "ʿalayya kadhā" as explicit, per the Tuḥfa. Without the lafẓ of Allāh, the phrase "ʿalayya kadhā" is treated as a kināya in some sources, but in the chapter on nadhr the relied-upon view per ʿUmdat al-Sālik and verified against al-Minhāj: this is ṣarīḥ.

Referenced scholars / texts

ʿUmdat al-Sālik, al-Minhāj, the Tuḥfa chapter on nadhr.

3. Sixth Fiqh Symposium of the Indian Fiqh Academy: Decisions 67–70

Background / Question

The session reviews the resolutions of the Sixth Fiqh Symposium of the Mujammaʿ al-Fiqh al-Islāmī (India), held at Dār al-ʿUlūm of the Islamic University at Maẓhar Fūr, ʿAẓam Garh (10–13 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 1428 / 30 March – 2 April 2007), attended by some 200 Indian scholars together with Dr. ʿUmar Ḥasan Kāsūlī (Brunei) and Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir al-ʿĀrifī (Zāhidān, Iran). Four topics were taken up. The Shaykh comments resolution by resolution from the Shāfiʿī viewpoint.

Ruling — Resolution 67: timing of stoning the Jamarāt (Ḥanafī-leaning text)

  • The Symposium urges pilgrims to follow the most cautious sunna in performing this rite, given its once-in-a-lifetime nature.
  • The Symposium says that on the 10th of Dhū al-Ḥijja, stoning before sunrise (after true Fajr) is makrūh for the general public; women, the disabled, the weak, and the sick may stone in that window without karāha.
  • Shāfiʿī correction: in the Shāfiʿī school, the time of Jamrat al-ʿAqaba on the 10th enters from the midpoint of the night, and the sunna is the prioritizing of the weak and women, not a position of karāha upon men. The relied-upon Shāfiʿī sources find no karāha for the general public when the weak stone in that window.
  • The Symposium adds: it is impermissible for anyone to stone before midnight of the 10th — because the time has not yet entered. (Agrees with Shāfiʿī starting point in substance.)
  • For the 11th and 12th: the Symposium fixes the start time at zawāl (sun's zenith) and extends until the true Fajr of the following day, urging stoning within these windows.
  • Shāfiʿī correction: these days' stoning enters at zawāl and extends until sunset of the last day of Tashrīq, not Fajr — and one may aggregate all days' stoning into the last day of Tashrīq if not detached, in order: first day's pebbles to the first pillar, then the second, then the third, then the second day's, then the third day's.
  • The Symposium adds: stoning before zawāl in the days of Tashrīq is permitted for a severe necessity by Abū Ḥanīfa's view; no dam is owed in that case.
  • Discussion: "severe necessity" was clarified to mean only what risks loss of life or limb, not mere need.
  • The Symposium states: on the 11th and 12th after sunset there is no karāha given current crowding.
  • Shāfiʿī correction: no karāha at all in our school, regardless of crowding, because the time runs from zawāl until sunset of the last day of Tashrīq, day and night included.
  • The Symposium states: if a pilgrim stays in Mina past sunset of the 12th and into Fajr of the 13th, he is obligated to stone on the 13th. This is the Ḥanafī position; in our school the situation differs.
  • Resolution on mabīt: it is sunna to spend the nights of Mina there; failure to do so means giving up a major sunna of Hajj — students should note that the Symposium treats mabīt of the days of Tashrīq as wājib in their school, while mabīt before ʿArafa is sunna and mabīt after ʿArafa (Tashrīq nights) is wājib per our school.

Ruling — Resolution 68: brain death and life support

  • If breathing has completely ceased and the signs of death are present, death is established. From that moment: will is executed, inheritance opens, ʿidda begins, etc.
  • If the patient is on a ventilator and doctors have not despaired and there is hope of recovery, the family may not disconnect unless continued treatment exceeds the patient's wealth and the heirs cannot bear the costs and have no other option.
  • Discussion (Shaykh's nuance): even with the patient's own money, treatment may be ceased if there are signs that the patient himself prefers his money preserved for his heirs over an extended ventilator dependence — the principle of ẓann al-riḍā. The heirs are obligated to cover only what is customary; extraordinary intervention may not be legally compelled.
  • If doctors have lost hope of return to natural breathing, the family may disconnect — paralleled by the Academy's earlier resolution on brain death (cessation of brain function with medical certainty of no return) which they classified as death and on which removing life support is permitted.
  • Distinguished case: a premature infant in a humidicrib. Prognosis is generally good, and the obligation to maintain the child is stronger; if the parent or guardian can afford it, the care is obligatory — unlike adult intensive support whose outcome is uncertain.

Ruling — Resolution 69: mercy killing

  • Active intervention that causes the patient's death — even to relieve unbearable pain, even to release the family from the burden of care — is murder, and is prohibited.
  • Withholding treatment a patient can in fact have, with the intention of hastening death, is also prohibited — even without administering a lethal drug.
  • Our school does not permit mercy killing even of an animal for its relief; the Mālikīs permit killing an animal for relief.
  • The Shaykh notes a position attributed to ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd al-Sharwānī permitting mercy killing of a human under three combined conditions: (a) unbearable, customarily intolerable pain, (b) certain imminent death (e.g., within half an hour), (c) the patient's consent. The Shaykh expresses reservation; this remains to be verified in Ḥāshiyat ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd.

Ruling — Resolution 70: Multi-Level / Network Marketing (MLM)

  • The current widespread forms of network marketing combine several sharʿī problems:
  • ghishsh (deception);
  • gharar (excessive uncertainty);
  • conditioning the sale on a separate matter unrelated to the sale's object;
  • combining two contracts in one, two sales in one;
  • features resembling qimār (gambling);
  • the buyer is not really seeking the product but the enticing recurring commissions.
  • Therefore joining such schemes is prohibited; pushing others into them and then drawing commissions from down-line is prohibited; Muslims should not engage in such trade.
  • Discussion: the Shaykh notes that since Shāfiʿīs and Ḥanafīs do not treat ḥiyal as inherently impermissible, an MLM structure stripped of gharar, ghishsh, and conditional linkage between purchase and commission could in principle be valid. One of the major MLM companies recently approached the Shaykh's father seeking a Shāfiʿī solution; he conditioned cooperation on first dropping the conditional structure linking purchase and commission. After some delay they returned agreeing to look at alternative product structures — a first such concession from any major company.

Referenced scholars / texts

Resolutions 67–70 of the Sixth Fiqh Symposium of the Mujammaʿ al-Fiqh al-Islāmī (India); the resolution of the Islamic Fiqh Academy on brain death; Ḥāshiyat ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd al-Sharwānī; the Shaykh's own remarks on Tuḥfa timings.

4. Closing Questions

Background / Question

Three questions from attendees on the first ten days of Dhū al-Ḥijja, on congregational voluntary night prayers, and on prostration after the prescribed prayers.

Ruling — Public takbīr during the first ten days of Dhū al-Ḥijja

  • Some scholars and madhāhib (and some of the salaf, and a view among the Ḥanbalīs) hold that takbīr is sunna publicly from the beginning of Dhū al-Ḥijja.
  • In our school, takbīr in the first ten days is not the relied-upon sunna; the takbīr mursal is on the days of Tashrīq only.
  • Application: in a place where the prevailing madhhab supports public takbīr from the start of Dhū al-Ḥijja, leave it alone; if visiting such a place, join them and take the dispensation. In a place that follows the Shāfiʿī school (e.g., Tarīm, Ḥaḍramawt, southern Yemen), introducing the practice would mix madhāhib and provoke fitna; it should not be done.

Ruling — Congregational performance of voluntary night prayers

  • The relied-upon Shāfiʿī position is that congregational performance of voluntary night prayer is not makrūh; it is preferred individually, but not disliked when in congregation.
  • If congregating helps people to actually pray the qiyām — which they would otherwise abandon out of laziness — any karāha in other madhāhib is set aside by the good intention. The blessed days and nights merit the encouragement.

Ruling — Sajda after the prescribed prayers

  • No prostration is legitimate except the prostration of recitation (sujūd al-tilāwa) and the prostration of thanksgiving (sujūd al-shukr).
  • Routine prostration after each obligatory prayer is not permitted in our school. Sujūd al-shukr requires a sudden, fresh blessing — completion of one's daily prayer is not such a blessing, nor are the ordinary, ongoing blessings of God's favor (otherwise one would remain in prostration for life).
  • Sajda after Witr is debated; the relied-upon view is that it is not performed. Two-cycle followed by repeating the Witr is also avoided (called naqḍ al-Witr).
  • If someone wishes to express thanks after a prayer, the proper response is to begin a separate ṣalāt al-shukr, not an independent prostration.
  • In Ḥanafī fiqh there may be wider scope for sujūd al-shukr — confirmed in some cases as expanding (Shaykh undertook to verify the reference). If the doer follows that madhhab, leave him; if he is Shāfiʿī, instruct him by way of irshād (guidance), not inkār (rebuke).

Referenced scholars / texts

The Minhāj and its commentaries; ʿUmdat al-Sālik; the Shāfiʿī chapter on stoning and mabīt; the Shaykh's own forthcoming references on sajda baʿd al-witr in the Ḥanafī tradition.
