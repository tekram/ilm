Book 1: Tuhfat al-Muhtaj, Kitab al-Nikah

1. Claiming the Witnesses Were Immoral

Ruling:

  • A claim that the witnesses were immoral is heard before the contract is concluded, even without explanation. After the contract is concluded it is not accepted unless the claimant explains the cause of the immorality, or the two spouses agree that the witnesses were immoral at the contract.
  • Their agreement is a joint acknowledgement and counts whether they say they knew then or only found out later, since the criterion is the reality.
  • This does not apply where they had earlier acknowledged before a judge that it was with upright witnesses and the judge ruled the marriage valid; then their later agreement is disregarded as to marital rights. The text adds that a bare acknowledgement without a judge's ruling may not suffice for this, though the earlier passage suggests it does, so this point needs to be checked. The teacher spelled out "marital rights" as the wife's maintenance and dowry and the husband's right of enjoyment.
  • Al-Adhra'i and others also hold an interdicted spendthrift woman's admission has no effect on her property; the same for a slave woman, as it is her master's right.
  • God's right is not affected by their agreement: if he triple-divorced her and they later agree, or prove, that the witnesses were immoral (or the guardian a minor), that does not remove the requirement of intervening marriage (tahlil); the divorce stands and she cannot return to him until another marriage. The teacher explained that the agreement only affects what is their own right, not God's.
  • If the defect really existed (the witnesses were in fact drinkers or committed major sins): inwardly the marriage was void even though outwardly they were presumed upright; between him and God he can renew the contract even after three divorces, since the first contract was invalid. But if the judge learns of what passed between them (marital relations without a valid contract) he separates them, with discipline; if the judge knew the first marriage was invalid he does not interfere with them (the passage here was partly garbled).
  • Al-Subki: the husband's proof that the witnesses were immoral is accepted if he does not want the marriage but wants to be rid of the dowry (e.g. after a triple divorce), provided he had not previously acknowledged its validity; likewise the wife's proof is accepted if she wants the dowry of the like after intercourse where it exceeds the named dowry, provided she had not affirmed validity. The commentators say this is sound where no earlier acknowledgement occurred.

2. The Witnesses' Own Statement

  • The witnesses' statement "we were immoral at the contract" has no effect on others, since they are acknowledging against someone else; but it has effect on themselves. Example: they attended their sister's contract, then she died and they inherited from her: the dowry drops (before consummation) or the named dowry is void (after) and the dowry of the like is due, if it does not exceed the named dowry, since they cannot create a right for themselves against others by their own acknowledgement.

3. If Only One Spouse Acknowledges

  • The husband acknowledges and the wife denies: they are separated, holding him to his statement. It is an annulment (fasakh) and does not reduce the count of divorces (another view: it is a divorce). He owes half the named dowry if there was no consummation, all of it if consummated. He does not inherit from her, since an acknowledgement is a limited proof that applies to him alone; but she inherits from him after swearing that he contracted with two upright witnesses. Al-Subki objected that both annulment and divorce presuppose a valid marriage, which he denies; the answer given: annulment is read as a ruling of invalidity and divorce as outward only. Al-Ramli's comparison with the slave woman case (a man marrying a slave while able to marry a free woman, then admitting it) is noted as a comparison that does not necessarily apply fully.
  • The wife acknowledges and the husband denies: she cannot separate them, since the marital bond is in his hand and the default is that it continues; but if he dies she does not inherit. If she dies or he divorces before consummation there is no dowry; after, she gets the lesser of the named dowry and the dowry of the like, unless she is interdicted for foolishness, whose acknowledgement has no effect on property. Al-Isnawi: dropping before consummation applies only if she has not yet received it; if she has, he cannot reclaim it.
  • If she says the contract was without guardian or witnesses and he says it was with them: she is believed on oath, since this denies the contract itself.
  • It is recommended to have the woman's consent witnessed where her consent is required.

Book 2: Fatawa al-Hindi (Islamic Fiqh Academy of India)

Resolution 110 (5/25) — Dialogue between religions: principles and etiquette

1. Interfaith dialogue is permitted on religious, social and political grounds, provided it does not touch Islam's creed and religious concepts, with the aims of tolerance, peaceful coexistence, calling to the faith, removing doubts, and solving social and political problems. The teacher: it is a call to Allah, but not at the expense of Islam; one need not recognize the validity of other religions, but only keep silent about them for peaceful coexistence. He added that many object to the plural "religions," since Islam is one religion, though the plural is acceptable in the sense that prophets brought legislation that differed in some branches.

2. Some values are shared across religions, so one may benefit from the books of other religions and refer to them when necessary. The teacher marked this as problematic: it is disputed between Ibn Hajar (permitted for those strong in knowledge and faith) and al-Ramli (not permitted at all), and a reader may transmit error or something Islam forbids.

3. It is not permitted to participate in the religious works and customs of other faiths, nor to give up permitted practices that have become part of inherited Islamic civilization, to avoid sedition. The teacher's examples of such practices: minarets, mosque decoration, the crescent as the sign of Islam, and the 'Id prayer; he and a student also noted the practice of broadcasting recitations or remembrance through loudspeakers before the dawn call or Friday in some Levantine countries, which he said should be dropped where it harms people, including Muslims.

4. Declaring tawhid and the Message before the world's nations and disavowing polytheism and disbelief is a duty, but care should be taken not to choose methods of disavowal that insult people of other faiths. The teacher said dialogue and discussion are wanted, seeking the truth, not rivalry.

5. Dialogue is an urgent need for building a sound human society, especially around shared social issues: poverty, illiteracy, immorality, abuse of employees, women and the elderly; Muslims must take part. The teacher added the first aim is the people's salvation from the Fire.

6. Dialogue between Muslims and political groups and religious organizations to protect Islamic religious, financial and social interests is not merely permitted but at times recommended.

7. Recommended steps: form a union of Muslim researchers qualified for dialogue; give special attention to the study of comparative religion in major universities and religious schools, with a department in each state; build relations with comparative-religion departments of universities; set up a forum of thinkers of different religions with periodic conferences; direct practical steps for dialogue with various institutions; promote the value of serving people among Muslims and encourage non-governmental charities. The teacher added that spending money on those who are weak can lead them to listen to the truth, since hearts are disposed to love those who do good to them.

  • Teacher's comments: he said that differences with other faiths are not like differences between schools but between truth and falsehood, Islam and disbelief, quoting Qur'an 98:6 ("those who disbelieved among the People of the Book and the polytheists will be in Hellfire, they are the worst of creatures"). He mentioned an online debater (Mu'adh 'Ulyan) who confronts Christians harshly and whose critics cite "do not argue with the People of the Book except in the best way" while overlooking "except those who wrong themselves"; he said the harshness answers what had been said against Islam.

The 26th Fiqh Seminar (Ujjain, March 2017)

Held 5-7 Jumada II 1438 (March 4-6, 2017), with over 300 scholars and jurists taking part. Topics: trade in gold and silver, environmental and noise pollution, benefiting from government projects, and buying and selling real estate.

Resolution 111 (1/26) — Trade in gold and silver (partly read)

1. Buying gold and silver with currencies is not counted as a sarf sale, so deferring one of the two counter-values is permitted. The teacher found this surprising and said it follows Ibn Taymiyya's view, whereas the other schools treat currencies as taking the place of gold and silver. A student explained that the Hanafis' cause of riba is weight and paper money is not weighed; he added that Mufti Taqi 'Uthmani (in Fiqh al-Buyu') allows salam in currencies, where the price is fixed but receipt delayed, while a currency still carries riba between its own kind.

2. Gold and silver may be bought and sold at more or less than the set price (the teacher: this is how people actually deal).

3. The sawdust and gold particles from a goldsmith's work may be set as the goldsmith's wage, provided the amount is not so unknown as to lead to dispute; but it is better to name a separate known wage.

4. Old gold or silver jewelry may not be exchanged for new with a difference; if necessary the owner should sell the old for currency and buy the new with the proceeds (the teacher noted the seminar did not follow Ibn Taymiyya here).

5. Gold and silver may not be bought and sold in a commodity exchange in a way that does not involve the buyer's receipt, such as only recording it on a computer, unless a specific quantity is set aside for him and actually received. The teacher said this is like selling "digital grams" that the seller does not own, which is invalid.

6. It is not permitted if it involves no real receipt of the item and price but only exchanges the price's rise and fall. A student compared it to Forex speculation on price indices rather than real buying and selling.

7. Storing gold and silver to wait for a rise is not counted as the forbidden hoarding (ihtikar), though the resolution advises avoiding it because it affects the value of other necessities; the teacher noted that on his school's view ihtikar applies only to food.

8. Smuggling is illegal, so buying gold by that route should be avoided, but a person who buys it that way owns it. The teacher said avoiding it is better, though a person may try to avoid paying what is unjustly taken from him.

9. Platinum is not gold, so the rulings of gold in contracts and Zakat do not apply to it.

The class stopped here.
