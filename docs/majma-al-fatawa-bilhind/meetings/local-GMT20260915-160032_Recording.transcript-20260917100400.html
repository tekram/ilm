Near-Pubescent Boys, Touching Foreign Women & Looking at Al-Amrad - Sep 15, 2026

📅 2026-09-15 🕐 16:00

Overview

This session from Majma' al-Fatawa bil-Hind covers rulings on physical interaction and looking (nazar) in the Shafi'i school, with detailed discussion from Tuhfat al-Muhtaj and its commentaries. Four main topics are addressed: the ruling on the near-pubescent boy (murahiq) relative to women's covering, permissible looking between men, the rules of touching foreign women, and the detailed ruling on looking at a beardless youth (amrad). The session includes comparative analysis across al-Ramli, Ibn Hajar, al-Rashidi, and Fath al-Mu'in.

1. The Near-Pubescent Boy (Murahiq) and Women's Covering

Background / Question

At what age is a boy treated like an adult man for purposes of a woman's obligation to cover (hijab) before him?

Ruling

  • The murahiq is one close to the age of puberty — approximately 14–15 years old (close to 15 per the stronger view; the range 10–12 yields a disputed middle case).
  • He is treated like a baligh (adult) in two matters:

1. Gaze (nazar): a woman must cover from him as she would from an adult man.

2. Khalwa (seclusion): seclusion with him is prohibited as with an adult man.

  • He is not like a baligh regarding entering homes: he may enter without explicit permission, except during the three private times (when women typically undress).
  • If he openly shows desire (tashawwuf) toward women: treated as an adult definitively.
  • His guardian must prevent him from looking at what is haram.
  • Imam al-Nawawi's position (referenced in commentaries): one who cannot distinguish male from female (severe intellectual disability) is treated like a mahram.

Four-category classification for children:

| Category | Rule |

|---|---|

| Very young child — cannot describe what he sees | Treated as non-existent; woman may uncover before him |

| Child who describes what he sees but without desire | Treated like a mahram |

| Child/adolescent who looks with desire, or murahiq | Treated like an adult man (baligh) |

| Baligh (adult) | Full prohibition applies |

Referenced scholars / texts

Ibn Hajar al-Haytami (Tuhfa), al-Nawawi, Ibn Qasim al-'Abbadi, Sharh al-Rawdh

2. Looking Between Men (Nazar al-Rajul ila al-Rajul)

Background / Question

What is the ruling on a man looking at another man?

Ruling

  • Permissible (by consensus) to look at another man with safety from fitnah and without desire.
  • Prohibited absolutely to look between the navel and knee — even from a mahram.
  • Ibn Hajar: the navel and knee themselves are included in the prohibition for looking outside prayer (though not in prayer).
  • Al-Ramli / al-Khatib: the navel and knee themselves are not 'awrah — only what is strictly between them.
  • The near-pubescent (murahiq) boy is treated like an adult both as a viewer and as one being viewed.

Referenced scholars / texts

Ibn Hajar al-Haytami, al-Ramli, al-Khatib al-Sharbini

3. Touching (Lams) Foreign Women

Background / Question

Can a man touch a foreign woman (ajnabiyya) with a barrier (ha'il)?

Ruling

  • Handshaking with a barrier is permissible, by analogy from the ruling that a man may touch another man's thigh with a barrier and safety from fitnah — derived by Shubra-Milisi and stated in the Tuhfa.
  • Condition added by Ibn Qasim al-'Abbadi: the touching must also be for a need (hajah), not merely permissible at will.
  • Al-Rashidi extends the principle: the mention of "handshake" in the text is just an example — the ruling applies to any form of contact with a foreign woman, for need, with barrier.
  • Touching the face (wajh):
  • Ibn Hajar's implied position (mafhum): touching the face or hands with a barrier + safety from fitnah may be permissible.
  • Fath al-Mu'in (explicit/mantouq): touching the face of a foreign woman is haram absolutely — even with barrier, even when looking is permitted (e.g., for marriage proposal or testimony).
  • Al-Rashidi (explicit): same as Fath al-Mu'in.
  • Conclusion: the explicit texts (mantouq) of al-Rashidi and Fath al-Mu'in outweigh Ibn Hajar's implied position (mafhum).
  • Touching parts other than face and hands from behind a barrier: haram even with safety from fitnah and absence of desire — agreed upon.
  • Barrier thickness matters per al-Ramli: a thin barrier (through which body heat is felt) may not be sufficient.

Three conditions for permissible handshake (concluded):

1. Presence of a barrier (ha'il)

2. Safety from fitnah

3. A genuine need (hajah)

Referenced scholars / texts

Ibn Hajar al-Haytami (Tuhfa), al-Rashidi, Fath al-Mu'in, Shubra-Milisi, Ibn Qasim al-'Abbadi, al-Ramli

4. Looking at the Beardless Youth (Al-Amrad)

Background / Question

Is it permissible to look at a beardless youth (amrad) without desire?

Ruling

  • Looking with desire: haram by ijma' — no dispute.
  • Looking without desire:
  • Stronger position (al-asahh al-mansus, adopted by Ibn Hajar and Shaykh al-Islam Zakariyya al-Ansari): still haram, because the amrad is a means to fitnah (like a woman — in fact, the Tuhfa says his case is graver since he is never permitted).
  • Al-Rafi'i's position: permissible without desire.
  • Debate: some scholars said treating the prohibition as the mu'tamad is the sound view of the madhhab; others said it is al-Nawawi's personal preference.

Defining "desire" (shawha) in this context:

  • Feeling a difference in one's soul between looking at the beardless youth and looking at a bearded man — finding the former more pleasing.
  • Even prolonged gazing without intention of intercourse constitutes sinful enjoyment.
  • Scholars have issued severe warnings; some salaf called beardless youths "effeminate ones" (inath) and recorded cases of scholars losing memorized Quran decades after such glances.

Exceptions (looking is permissible):

  • If the amrad is the viewer's mahram (by blood, milk, or marriage): permissible as with any mahram.
  • Owner may look at a male slave or eunuch under the same conditions (safety from fitnah, no desire).

Referenced scholars / texts

Ibn Hajar al-Haytami (Tuhfa), al-Rafi'i, al-Nawawi, Shaykh al-Islam Zakariyya al-Ansari, al-Ramli, Ibn Qasim al-'Abbadi, al-Ghazali (Ihya' — on the grave warnings)

5. 'Awrah of the Female Slave (Ama)

Background / Question

Is the female slave's 'awrah the same as a free woman's?

Ruling

  • Most correct view among the muhaqqiqun (adopted in Tuhfa): the ama is like the hurra — she must cover from head to foot, same as a free woman. Reason: shared womanhood and risk of fitnah; many slave women surpass free women in beauty.
  • Counter-view (practice of people): ama's 'awrah is like a man's — only navel to knee. This is what people typically act on.
  • 'Umar's action of striking a slave woman for covering like a free woman: does not prove the ruling — may have been because she was misleading people into thinking she was a free woman.
  • Al-Bulqini challenged the Tuhfa's claim that this is the view of "the muhaqqiqun," calling it unrecognized.
  • Conclusion: the Tuhfa's position stands as mu'tamad; treating the ama like the hurra is more cautious (ahwat).

Referenced scholars / texts

Ibn Hajar al-Haytami (Tuhfa), al-Bulqini, al-Mughni, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (ra), al-Adhra'i
