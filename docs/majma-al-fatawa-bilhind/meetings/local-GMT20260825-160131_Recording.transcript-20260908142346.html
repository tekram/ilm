Rulings on Looking Before Marriage & Bay' al-Wafa' - August 25, 2026

📅 2026-08-25 🕐 16:01

Overview

This Majma' al-Fatawa Bilhind session opens with the continuation of a Tuhfat al-Muhtaj-style commentary on the fiqh of nazar (looking at a prospective spouse before marriage), then moves into a reading of the Islamic Fiqh Academy's (Majma' al-Fiqh al-Islami) resolution on bay' al-wafa' (sale with a right of redemption) and related lending/pledge questions.

1. Looking at a Prospective Spouse (Nazar)

Background / Question

The lesson continues the Shafi'i ruling that a man may look at a woman he intends to marry, provided three conditions are met: he intends marriage to her specifically, he has reasonable hope of a favorable answer, and he knows she is free of a marriage or a waiting period ('idda) that would make even indirect allusion (ta'rid) to marriage forbidden to her (e.g., a revocably-divorced woman, as opposed to one irrevocably divorced or widowed, for whom allusion is permitted).

Ruling

  • The look is recommended (sunnah) before the formal proposal (khitba); scholars differ on whether it remains recommended after the proposal (Ibn Hajar and al-Khatib: no, because refusal afterward may cause hurt; al-Ramli: yes, it remains sunnah).
  • It is preferable that the woman not know she is being looked at, since awareness may lead her to adorn herself in ways that defeat the purpose of the look (though some hold it should be with her knowledge).
  • Cosmetics: covering visible, minor blemishes (e.g., light freckles) to appear naturally radiant is permissible; using make-up to conceal a defect a suitor would object to (e.g., vitiligo) is a form of deceptive misrepresentation (ghish) and impermissible.
  • The look may be repeated as needed to be reasonably confident, but once satisfied, further looking is prohibited as no longer serving the permitted purpose.
  • The man may look at her face and hands only; she is likewise recommended to look at him (beyond his 'awra) since she may find a defect she would not accept either.
  • A photograph may substitute for direct viewing under the same rules of repetition/sufficiency; repeated viewing of a photo is fine if it does not provoke desire (shahwa) after one has already been "satisfied."
  • If direct viewing isn't possible, it is recommended to send a trusted relative (mahram) to view and describe her, even features not normally permissible to describe, since indirect benefit is better than none.
  • Conversation between the prospective spouses is not itself forbidden (speech is not 'awra) provided there is no seclusion (khalwa), no flirtatious tone, and no calls/messaging that functions as virtual seclusion; discussing intimate matters or the other's past relationships is inappropriate.
  • Regional custom varies widely (full veiling and no viewing in some communities, informal viewing arranged through mothers in others); in Tarim, the vast majority historically married without ever seeing the spouse beforehand.
  • If displeased after viewing, the suitor should stay silent rather than announce refusal outright, since prolonged silence signals withdrawal gently without embarrassing the family.

Referenced scholars / texts

Ibn Hajar al-Haytami (Tuhfat al-Muhtaj), al-Ramli, al-Khatib al-Shirbini, Ibn Abd al-Salam, al-Adhra'i.

2. Bay' al-Wafa' and Related Rulings (Fiqh Academy Resolution)

Background / Question

Reading from a fatwa-assembly resolution (paralleling material from Sheikh Taqi Uthmani's comparative fiqh work) addressing debt culture, pledges (rahn), and bay' al-wafa' (a sale conditioned on the buyer returning the item once the price is repaid — equivalent to bay' al-'ahda in the Hadhrami Shafi'i tradition).

Ruling

  • The assembly laments the weakening of mutual cooperation (qard hasan) and the spread of debt-delay (matl), urging Muslims to avoid unnecessary debt (permissible only for genuine needs like medical treatment, a daughter's marriage, or housing — not luxury or business ventures funded by risky debt).
  • A pledge (rahn) is security only; the creditor may not benefit from the pledged property, as that would constitute a loan that draws benefit (riba).
  • If a creditor does benefit from pledged property, the value of that benefit must be deducted from the debt, and the property returned without further claim.
  • A person in severe need of cash may sell an item to another with an informal intention to repurchase it later (bay' al-wafa'/bay' al-'ahda); this is permitted, though the second purchase should not be stipulated explicitly within the same contract — only as a mutual understanding or promise.
  • A deposit taken under the name of "security" in a property lease is legally treated as a loan.
  • Reducing rent below fair market value in a way that amounts to a benefit tied to a loan (qard jarra naf'an) falls under the prohibition of riba.

Referenced scholars / texts

Sheikh Taqi Uthmani (comparative fiqh of transactions), the Fiqh Assembly resolution on bay' al-wafa'.
